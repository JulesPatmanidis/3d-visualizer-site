# 3d-visualizer

A volume and vector field renderer compiled to WebAssembly, running on WebGL2.
Volume rendering (MIP, isosurface, compositing, slicing) with bricking and empty
space skipping, plus vector field hedgehogs, stream and path lines, and LIC.

Live at https://julespatmanidis.github.io/3d-visualizer-site/

Desktop browsers only. Float textures need `EXT_color_buffer_float` and
`OES_texture_float_linear`.

## What is in this repo

Build output and data, pushed here by CI from a private source repo. Nothing in
it is edited by hand.

The source is closed because it grew out of TU Delft coursework and publishing a
solution would hand it to the next cohort. The assignment skeleton is TU Delft's.
The renderer, the restructuring and the web port are mine.

## Data

**Hurricane Isabel.** The authors will like to thank Bill Kuo, Wei Wang, Cindy
Bruyere, Tim Scheitlin, and Don Middleton of the U.S. National Center for
Atmospheric Research (NCAR) and the U.S. National Science Foundation (NSF) for
providing the Weather Research and Forecasting (WRF) Model simulation data of
Hurricane Isabel. Distributed for the IEEE Visualization 2004 contest, which asks
for that acknowledgement and states no other terms. The set here is a 500x500
slice over the full 48 timesteps.

**Block, DNS slice.** A slice of a Direct Numerical Simulation of flow around a
block, generated by R.W.C.P. Verstappen and A.E.P. Veldman of the University of
Groningen. The technique is described in R.W.C.P. Verstappen and A.E.P. Veldman,
1998, *Spectro-consistent discretization of Navier-Stokes: a Challenge to RANS
and LES*, Journal of Engineering Mathematics, Vol. 34, pp. 163-179. The data is
supplied on the condition that they are credited, which is what this paragraph
is for.

**Tube.** Supplied with the course in the same format as the block data, with no
provenance given.

**Gulf Stream** and **Agulhas Retroflection.** Surface fields from the HYCOM
ESPC-D-V02 global 1/12 degree ocean analysis, pulled through its NetCDF subset
service. Both are 326x351 points over 62 hourly steps, a little over two and a
half days, the Gulf Stream from 2 August 2026 and the Agulhas from 1 August. The
Gulf Stream box runs 31 to 45 north and 76 to 50 west, the Agulhas box 30 to 44
south and 12 to 38 east. Eastward and northward velocity are the vector, sea
surface temperature is the first scalar and sea surface salinity the second. Funding for the development of HYCOM has been
provided by the National Ocean Partnership Program and the Office of Naval
Research, and data assimilative products using HYCOM are funded by the U.S.
Navy.

**The scalar volumes** (bonsai, tooth, carp, pig, stent, orange, tomato,
backpack) came with the course. They are the volume rendering sets that circulate
widely in the field, and the course material named no origin for them.

**The remaining volumes** come from Pavol Klacansky's Open Scientific
Visualization Datasets, https://klacansky.com/open-scivis-datasets/, which
collects them from the sources credited below. Two were altered to fit in a
browser. The stag beetle is box filtered to half resolution on every axis, from
832x832x494 down to 416x416x247, keeping the original 12 bit value scale. The
turbulence field was 32 bit float (not supported by the 3d viewer) so it was
quantised to 16 bits with the top 0.1% of values clipped.
